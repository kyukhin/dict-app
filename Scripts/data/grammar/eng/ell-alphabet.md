# The alphabet, the stress mark and the diaeresis

Greek has 24 letters. Seven are vowels: α, ε, η, ι, ο, υ, ω. η, ι and υ sound "i", as in machine; ο and ω sound "o". Σ is written ς at the end of a word: *σας*. Some pairs of letters make one sound: see the second table.

Every word of two or more syllables has a stress mark, the *τόνος*, on its stressed vowel: *μήλο*, *καφές*. On a capital it stands before the letter: *Άννα*. It can change the meaning: *πότε*, when, but *ποτέ*, never.

The diaeresis, *διαλυτικά*, shows that two vowels are said apart, not as a pair: *προϊόν* (pro-i-ón), *Αϊτή* (a-i-tí). With the stress on the first vowel, none is needed: *τσάι* (tsá-i), *ρολόι* (ro-ló-i).

## Examples

- Πού είναι το μουσείο;
  Where is the museum?
- Ο παππούς μου είναι γέρος, αλλά γερός.
  My grandfather is old, but strong.
- Θέλεις τσάι ή καφέ;
  Would you like tea or coffee?
- Αυτό το προϊόν είναι ευρωπαϊκό.
  This product is European.

## Watch out

- Words of one syllable have no stress mark: *και*, *το*, *θα*. The exceptions are *ή*, or, and the question words *πού* and *πώς*: *Πού μένεις;*, Where do you live? Words written all in capitals have none either: *ΑΘΗΝΑ*.
- The Greek question mark looks like an English semicolon: *Τι κάνεις;*, How are you?
